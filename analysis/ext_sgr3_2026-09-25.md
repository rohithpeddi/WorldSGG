# SGR3-style retrieval inside WorldRAG: results (2026-09-25)

Design, bank, deviations and how to reproduce: [setup/EXT_SGR3_RETRIEVAL.md](../setup/EXT_SGR3_RETRIEVAL.md).
Branch `exp/ext-sgr3`. Every arm uses Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with standard decoding
(T = 0.2, `max_new_tokens` 128) on predcls, on the 150-video list `test_worldbbox_thinking150.txt`,
with the same prompt, visual input and GT object inventory. The retrieved context is capped at
**B = 90 tokens**, which is the mean of R1's context. All runs have 150/150 pkls, 25,755
(frame, object) predictions each, parse rate 1.0, and no `raw_response=None`. Scored with
`lib/mllm/eval/score_run.py` (stock WorldSGG evaluator, all frames, Full GT).

## Findings

1. **Sampling noise is at most about 0.5 points.** R0 and R0b differ by 0.0 to 0.5 in every column
   (OU-nt mR 53.1 vs 53.4). The other arms are single draws, so treat gaps under ~1 point as ties.

2. **Our text retrieval (R1) does not help Qwen3-VL-8B with standard decoding. It is at or below no
   context everywhere.** Compared with R0/R0b, R1 is −1.8 wc mR@20, −1.2 to −1.6 OO mR, and −1.3 to
   −1.6 OU-nt mR. Capping it at B (R1@B) changes nothing. One reason: the top-1 node barely depends
   on the queried object. There are only 2.3 distinct contexts per frame on average, with 4.4
   queried objects per video (1–10), so the "retrieved" context is close to one generic
   description per video.

3. **SGR3-style reference graphs raise recall a lot, and mean recall only on observed objects.**
   R2-3 against R0:
   - recall: +4.9 wc R@20, +5.5 nc R@50, +3.7 OU-nt R@50;
   - mean recall: +1.2 to +1.7 nc mR@50, +2.0 to +2.4 OO mR@50;
   - OU-nt mR: −1.3 to −1.6 (51.8 vs 53.1/53.4).

   k = 3 is the best k. k = 5 falls on every mR column, where the budget is split into five
   fragments of about 12 tokens each.

4. **Most of the R2 gain is not retrieval: a random train scene graph gets most of it.** Here is
   what the retrieved scene adds over R2-rand at k = 1:

   | Column | R2-1 − R2-rand |
   |---|---:|
   | wc R@20 | +0.9 |
   | wc mR@20 | +0.5 |
   | nc mR@50 | +1.0 |
   | OO mR | **+2.4** |
   | OU-nt R@50 | −0.1 |
   | OU-nt mR | **−0.7** |

   So visual retrieval helps observed objects a little and does nothing for unobserved ones.
   This fits the retrieval diagnostic: the top-1 reference scene contains the queried object's
   class for only 19.2% of GT objects, against 11.4% for a random scene. SigLIP2 similarity across
   *different homes* mostly matches room appearance, not the objects in it.

5. **The mechanism is label-vocabulary exposure.** Showing edges in the AG vocabulary stops the
   model from answering with labels outside the list, which score nothing. From
   `lib/mllm/tools/sgr3_pred_dist.py`:

   | Share of labels | R0 | R1 | R2-1 | R2-rand | R2-3 |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | `unknown` (out-of-vocabulary), contacting | 20.2% | 17.8% | 3.6% | 3.8% | 2.4% |
   | `unknown` (out-of-vocabulary), spatial | 7.8% | 9.7% | 1.6% | 1.7% | 1.1% |
   | `not_contacting`, contacting | 14.6% | 21.5% | 33.4% | 31.9% | 38.8% |

   Spatial labels per pair also go from 1.10 to 1.21–1.25. That lifts R and the head predicates, and
   explains why mR moves less and OU-nt mR (more tail) drops. The RAG × thinking run is already at
   3.2% contacting / 3.7% spatial out-of-vocabulary labels: thinking decoding removes most of this
   failure on its own.

6. **The hybrid (R3) is worse than R2 alone.** Giving half the budget to R1's context removes about
   half of R2's gain and does not improve OU-nt.

7. **Budget control holds.** R2/R3 contexts never exceed 90 tokens, and their means are 82–89
   against R1's 90.3. R1@B is the strictly capped R1: mean 74, since 46% of its prompts are
   truncated. Padding from the same reference video made up for short top-3-frame graphs (R2-1
   p5 = 48 tokens, where one scene runs out of edges).

## Leakage

`/data3/rohith/ag/cache/sgr3/leakage.json`: 7,516 bank videos from `world4d_rel_annotations/train`.
The bank's overlap with `test_worldbbox_1511` is **0**, and with the AG test split (1,750) is
**0**. This is asserted at build time, at query time (including the 150 query ids) and at runner
load time.

## Against the pre-registered decision rule

The rule reads: "If R2 beats R1 at the fixed budget, adopt R2 in WorldRAG for Tracks A and B."

- **The letter of the rule is met.** R2-3 beats R1 and R1@B on 9 of 10 columns and ties OU-nt mR
  (51.8 vs 51.8/52.1).
- **The spirit is not.** The gain comes from putting an AG-vocabulary reference graph in the prompt,
  not from SGR3's visual retrieval (finding 4). The same arm is also *below* no context on OU-nt mR,
  the column the paper's retrieval claim rests on.

If R2 is adopted for Tracks A/B, report it as "reference-graph prompting". Also include R2-rand and
R0 rows. Otherwise a reviewer can attribute the gain to retrieval when it is mostly vocabulary
grounding, which a label list or a random exemplar gives too.

## Thinking arm (~30 GPU-h): not launched, and not recommended now

- The R2 gain is mostly the out-of-vocabulary fix, and thinking decoding already has it: RAG ×
  thinking has 3.2% `unknown` contacting labels, against 17.8% for R1 with standard decoding.
- Retrieval proper adds nothing on OU-nt with standard decoding.
- The expected OU-nt gain of R2-3 × thinking over RAG × thinking (53.8) is therefore about zero,
  and 30 GPU-h would mainly buy an OO/R gain.

A cheaper check, if wanted: R2-3 × thinking on a 30-video subset (~6 GPU-h), compared with RAG ×
thinking on the same 30 videos. Run the full 150 only if OU-nt mR gains 1 point or more there.
